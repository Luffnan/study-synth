## The Economic and Business Environment

### The Five-Sector Circular Flow Model

#### Overview of the Circular Flow

- The circular flow of income shows the connections between the five sectors of an economy: consumers (households), businesses (firms), financial institutions, the government, and the overseas sector.
- The model shows where money is injected into the economy and where money leaks out of the economy.
- Economists use injections and leakages to calculate changes in the level of economic activity within an economy.
- When injections are greater than leakages, economic growth occurs and the economy expands.
- When leakages are greater than injections, the economy experiences economic decline.
- It is the government's role to manage and maintain balance within the economy by altering money flows and influencing decisions within sectors.

#### Consumer and Business Sectors (Two-Sector Model)

- The household sector is made up of consumers, who hold economic resources such as land, labour, capital and enterprise.
- In the simple two-sector model, households sell their resources to firms in exchange for income (wages, rent, interest, profit).
- Firms use household resources to produce goods and services — this is known as production.
- Households use their income to buy goods and services from firms — this is known as consumption.
- Consumers and businesses are interdependent: businesses rely on consumer demand and consumers rely on businesses for goods, services and income.
- The two-sector model is in equilibrium when income (Y) = consumption (C).

#### Financial Sector (Third Sector)

- The financial sector refers to financial institutions such as banks that act as intermediaries between savers and borrowers.
- Financial institutions receive savings from individuals and businesses and lend money to those who need to borrow.
- Saving (S) is putting money away for later use and is a leakage from the circular flow of income.
- Investment (I) is when money is borrowed and used to expand and grow a business, and is an injection into the circular flow of income.

#### Government Sector (Fourth Sector)

- The government sector includes local, state and federal governments.
- Taxation (T) is a leakage — governments collect taxes from individuals and businesses when they earn an income or profit.
- Government expenditure (G) is an injection — governments spend tax revenue on infrastructure, welfare payments, education and health.

#### Overseas Sector (Fifth Sector)

- The overseas sector accounts for global (macroeconomic) influences upon a nation's economy and explains the role of trade in helping an economy grow.
- Trade consists of exports and imports.
- Exports (X) are Australian businesses selling goods or services to overseas individuals, businesses or governments — an injection into the circular flow.
- Imports (M) are the buying of overseas goods or services by Australians — a leakage from the circular flow.
- Leakages from the economy are Savings (S), Taxation (T) and Imports (M).
- Injections into the economy are Investment (I), Government expenditure (G) and Exports (X).

### The Business Cycle

#### Nature of the Business Cycle

- The Australian economy experiences a cycle of 'booms' and 'busts' — periods of high and low economic activity called the business cycle.
- After a period of prosperity (boom), business activity gradually slows until a recession or depression is reached, after which business picks up again until prosperity is restored.
- A recession is when an economy gets smaller for a period of six months or longer.
- A depression is a long and severe recession.
- The level of economic activity fluctuates — total production, incomes, spending and employment rise and fall.
- Fluctuations are caused by changes in total spending: consumer spending (consumption), business spending (investment), government spending and exports.

#### Contraction Phase

- Falling levels of production (output).
- Decreasing consumer spending.
- Rate of inflation may fall.
- Wage rates generally fall.
- Interest rates eventually fall.
- Level of unemployment rises.

#### Expansion Phase

- Rising levels of production (output).
- Increasing consumer spending.
- Rate of inflation may rise.
- Wage rates generally rise.
- Interest rates eventually rise.
- Level of unemployment falls.

## Key Terms

**Economy** — The system of producing, distributing and consuming goods and services within a society.

**Production** — When firms use the resources of households to produce goods and services.

**Consumption** — When households use their income to buy goods and services.

**Interdependent** — When sectors of the economy rely on each other to function successfully.

**Household sector** — Consumers who hold economic resources such as land, labour, capital and enterprise.

**Firms sector** — Businesses that use household resources to produce goods and services.

**Financial sector** — Financial institutions such as banks that act as intermediaries between savers and borrowers.

**Saving (S)** — Putting money away for later use; a leakage from the circular flow of income.

**Investment (I)** — Money borrowed and used to expand and grow a business; an injection into the circular flow.

**Taxation (T)** — Money collected by governments from individuals and businesses; a leakage from the circular flow.

**Government expenditure (G)** — Government spending on items like infrastructure, welfare, education and health; an injection into the circular flow.

**Exports (X)** — Australian goods or services sold to overseas buyers; an injection into the circular flow.

**Imports (M)** — Overseas goods or services bought by Australians; a leakage from the circular flow.

**Leakages** — Money flowing out of the circular flow of income — Savings, Taxation and Imports (S + T + M).

**Injections** — Money flowing into the circular flow of income — Investment, Government expenditure and Exports (I + G + X).

**Two-sector equilibrium** — Income (Y) = Consumption (C)

**Income (Y)** — Y = wages + rent + interest + profit

**Economic growth condition** — Injections (I + G + X) > Leakages (S + T + M)

**Economic decline condition** — Leakages (S + T + M) > Injections (I + G + X)

**Business cycle** — The pattern of fluctuating periods of high and low economic activity — booms and busts.

**Recession** — When an economy gets smaller (contracts) for a period of six months or longer.

**Depression** — A long and severe recession.

**Boom** — A peak period of high economic activity and prosperity in the business cycle.

**Contraction** — A phase of the business cycle marked by falling output, spending and rising unemployment.

**Expansion** — A phase of the business cycle marked by rising output, spending and falling unemployment.
