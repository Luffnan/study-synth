## The Economic and Business Environment

### The Five-Sector Circular Flow Model

#### Purpose of the Circular Flow

- The circular flow of income shows the connections between the five sectors of an economy: consumers, businesses, financial institutions, the government and the overseas sector.
- It shows where money is injected into the economy and where money is leaked from it.
- Injections and leakages help economists calculate changes in the level of economic activity within an economy.

#### The Consumer and Business Sectors

- The household sector is made up of consumers who hold economic resources: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
- In a simple two-sector model, households sell their resources to firms (businesses) in exchange for an income (wages, rent, interest, profit).
- Firms use these resources to produce goods and services — this is known as production.
- Households use their income to buy goods and services — this is known as consumption.
- Consumers and businesses are interdependent: businesses rely on consumers to buy their goods, and consumers rely on businesses for goods, services and income.
- The two-sector model is in equilibrium when income (Y) = consumption (C).

#### The Financial Sector (Third Sector)

- The financial sector refers to financial institutions such as banks that act as intermediaries between savers and borrowers.
- They receive savings from individuals and businesses and lend this money to others who need to borrow.
- Saving (S) refers to putting money away for later use and is a leakage from the circular flow of income.
- Investment (I) is when money is borrowed and used to expand and grow a business — it is an injection into the circular flow of income.

#### The Government Sector (Fourth Sector)

- The government sector refers to local, state and federal governments.
- Taxation (T) is a leakage: governments collect taxes from individuals and businesses when they earn an income or profit.
- Government expenditure (G) is an injection: governments spend money raised through taxation on items such as infrastructure, welfare payments, education and health.

#### The Overseas Sector

- Including the overseas sector creates a macroeconomic model that details global influences on a nation's economy.
- It explains the role of trade in helping an economy grow; trade consists of exports and imports.
- Exports (X) are Australian businesses selling goods or services to overseas individuals, businesses or governments — an injection into the circular flow.
- Imports (M) are the buying of overseas goods or services by Australians — a leakage from the circular flow.

#### Injections, Leakages and Economic Activity

- Injections into the economy are investment (I), government expenditure (G) and exports (X).
- Leakages out of the economy are savings (S), taxation (T) and imports (M).
- The circular flow of income is used to measure whether an economy is growing or shrinking by comparing injections and leakages.
- When injections are greater than leakages, economic growth occurs and the economy expands.
- When leakages are greater than injections, the economy experiences economic decline.
- The government's role is to manage and maintain a balance in the economy by altering flows of money and influencing decisions within sectors.
- The effect of injections and leakages is to raise or lower the level of economic activity.

### The Business Cycle

#### Nature of the Business Cycle

- The Australian economy experiences cycles of 'booms' and 'busts' — periods of high and low economic activity referred to as the business cycle.
- After a period of prosperity, business activity gradually slows until a recession or depression is reached.
- A recession is when an economy gets smaller for a period of six months or longer.
- A depression is a long and severe recession.
- Eventually business picks up again until prosperity is restored, completing the cycle.
- The level of economic activity fluctuates: total production, incomes, spending and employment rise and fall.
- Fluctuations are caused by changes in total spending — consumer spending (consumption), business spending (investment), government spending and exports.

#### Phases of the Business Cycle

- The main phases shown on the business cycle graph are boom, contraction, recession, expansion and depression.
- Contraction key features: falling levels of production (output); decreasing consumer spending; rate of inflation may fall; wage rates generally fall; interest rates eventually fall; level of unemployment rises.
- Expansion key features: rising levels of production (output); increasing consumer spending; rate of inflation may rise; wage rates generally rise; interest rates eventually rise; level of unemployment falls.

## Key Terms

**Economy** — The system of production, consumption and exchange of money, goods and services within a nation.

**Production** — When firms use the resources of households to produce goods and services.

**Consumption (C)** — When households use their income to buy goods and services.

**Interdependent** — Reliance of sectors on each other — businesses need consumers to buy goods, and consumers need businesses for goods, services and income.

**Saving (S)** — Putting money away for later use; a leakage from the circular flow of income.

**Investment (I)** — Money borrowed and used to expand and grow a business; an injection into the circular flow of income.

**Taxation (T)** — Money collected by governments from individuals and businesses on income or profit; a leakage from the circular flow.

**Government expenditure (G)** — Government spending of money raised through taxation on items such as infrastructure, welfare, education and health; an injection into the circular flow.

**Exports (X)** — Australian businesses selling goods or services to overseas buyers; an injection into the circular flow.

**Imports (M)** — Goods or services bought by Australians from overseas; a leakage from the circular flow.

**Income (Y)** — Earnings such as wages, rent, interest and profit received by households for supplying economic resources.

**Two-sector equilibrium** — Y = C (income equals consumption).

**Business cycle** — The cycle of booms and busts — periods of high and low economic activity in an economy.

**Recession** — A period when the economy gets smaller for six months or longer.

**Depression** — A long and severe recession.

**Economic growth** — Occurs when injections into the economy are greater than leakages, causing the economy to expand.

**Economic decline** — Occurs when leakages from the economy are greater than injections.
